# Producer sonic signatures — techhouse R31 (+ scored alternates)

Web-verified deep-dives (2026-07-15 research pass) behind the six
`producer_style` palettes. THE SIX: Dom Dolla (`bassled`), Purple Disco
Machine (`discofunk`), HUGEL (`latin`), MK (`pianohouse`), Fred again..
(`lofi`), David Guetta (`bigroom`). ALTERNATES kept for future swaps:
Calvin Harris, Disco Lines, Peggy Gou — and Sammy Virji, BANKED as the
anchor pick for the UK Garage genre pass (his 2-step identity belongs
there, not inside techhouse's four-on-the-floor).

### David Guetta
BPM: 125-128 BPM (future rave 126-130; the pop crossovers sit ~124-128; 'I'm Good (Blue)' ~125). For the kids' bucket target 124-126.
DRUMS: Four-on-the-floor at ~125 BPM: a tight, punchy modern-EDM kick with solid sub support (not a boomy 909 tail — short, controlled, festival-clean). Crisp closed hats strictly on the offbeats, occasional 16th shuffles for lift into transitions; sparse claps/snares on 2 & 4 that get bigger (layered clap + short bright snare) in the drop. Almost no swing — the groove is dead-straight and machine-driven; propulsion comes from offbeat synth stabs, not percussion syncopation. Big snare/clap rolls (8ths → 16ths → 32nds) in the final bars of every build.
BASS: Two modes he alternates: (1) rolling offbeat/8th-note bass locked between kicks — a saturated analog-style saw/reese with filter-LFO movement (the future rave engine room), heavily sidechained to the kick so the whole low end pumps; (2) in the pop crossovers, a simpler bouncy octave/root 8th-note bass doubling the chord roots. Always mono below ~120 Hz, always ducking to the kick — the pump IS the feel.
CHORDS/PADS: Wide sidechained supersaw pad stacks playing 4-chord loops (minor-leaning pop progressions: i–VI–III–VII / vi–IV–I–V territory), voiced with sus2/add9 extensions for that trance lift. Under the pads: piano-house or pluck-stab chord hits on offbeats (the 'I'm Good' skank). Everything breathes with the kick via sidechain compression — the pumping pad bed is non-negotiable to the sound.
LEAD/HOOK: THE signature: a huge detuned supersaw STAB RIFF playing the vocal melody instrumentally in the drop — short, punchy, saturated saw stacks (6-8 detuned oscillators), multiband-compressed and lightly distorted, playing a Eurodance-nostalgia earworm (Eiffel 65 'Blue' melody, Haddaway, Alphaville). The drop is call-and-response: gritty bass motif answers the soaring saw lead. Secondary hooks: bright piano chords or plucks restating the topline in breakdowns before the saws take over.
FX/TEXTURE: Long filtered builds — the breakdown restates the melody on piano/pads, then a low-pass filter opens over 8-16 bars while a white-noise riser + snare roll + pitch-riser stack tension; a 1-beat silence or reverse-cymbal gap right before the drop; big reverb-tail impact/crash on beat 1 of the drop. Uplifters/downlifters everywhere, glossy wide reverb on pads, tight slap-delay on lead stabs. Everything polished and loud — zero lo-fi grit in the pop crossovers.
MOOD: Big-room euphoria: verse (pumping pads + light drums) → filtered build → explosive saw-stab drop → breakdown to emotional piano/pad restatement → bigger second drop. High energy but glossy and celebratory, never aggressive — the tension is anticipation, not menace. Nostalgic-anthemic: melodies you already know, supersized.
HOOK-ID: The huge detuned-supersaw STAB RIFF that sings a nostalgic Eurodance melody as the drop — pumping sidechained pads underneath, offbeat rolling bass answering it, after a long filtered piano-breakdown build. If a track drops into short saturated saw stabs playing an earworm-you-already-know over a dead-straight 126 BPM four-on-the-floor with the whole mix breathing to the kick, it's Guetta.

### Dom Dolla
BPM: 123-130 BPM; sweet spot 125-128 (girl$ ~126, Take It/San Frandisco ~123-124, Saving Up 130). Fits the app's 120-128 house bucket at ~126.
DRUMS: Clean, punchy, round 4/4 house kick — tight and controlled, never distorted or rumbling; tuned for club-system impact but reads 'bouncy' not 'heavy'. Crisp closed 16th hats with an offbeat open hat on the 'and', textural layering on hats/percussion (he layers noise/texture onto hats for character — Devious Machines Texture). Tight clap/snare on 2 and 4 with a short dry room. Sparse shaker/conga-type loop percussion. Micro-swing only (~54-58% swing feel, groove from syncopation not shuffle); the pocket comes from kick-bass call-and-response, not swung hats. Arrangement is disciplined: elements get space, one new layer at a time.
BASS: THE signature element. Rubbery, bouncy, syncopated filtered bassline that ducks around the kick (off-beat/16th-note stabs, call-and-response with the kick — never a static rolling 8th pattern). Sound: FM or saw-based mid-bass (Serum) layered over a saturated sine/sub layer; grit and top-end added deliberately so it cuts on small speakers (his stated 'secret sauce' is Ableton Erosion on basslines); movement from low-pass filter modulation — the recognizable 'wobble'/talkbox-like rubberiness — plus LFO-tool sidechain pump locked to the kick. The bass riff is frequently the melodic hook itself (San Frandisco, girl$).
CHORDS/PADS: Sparse but present — he self-describes as chord-driven. Short filtered stab chords and plucks (U-he Hive 2 pluck/stab patches), minor-key piano/disco stabs on the Euro-disco-leaning cuts (Saving Up), occasional warm filtered-disco loop or organ stab. Voicings: tight minor 7th/9th stabs, rhythmically placed on offbeats, heavily sidechained. Pads are rare and low in the mix — space is left for bass + vocal hook; when present, a soft filtered pad swells only in breakdowns.
LEAD/HOOK: Pitched/formant-shifted vocal phrases carry the hook — short chopped vocal riffs pitched down to an 'alien'/rubbery talkbox-like character (Take It) or pitched up playfully, often doubling or answering the bass riff. Hook architecture = vocal earworm + bass riff as one interlocking unit. For an instrumental palette: replace the vocal with a formant-filtered/talkbox-style synth lead or vowel-filtered pluck playing a 1-2 bar riff, trading phrases with the bass. Secondary hooks: siren-like motifs (San Frandisco) and single-note piano/stab accents. Vocals kept smooth with dynamic resonance suppression (Soothe 2) — never harsh.
FX/TEXTURE: Restrained, musical FX: long low-pass/high-pass filter sweeps into and out of sections (tension by subtraction — filtering the whole groove down then snapping back), short white-noise risers, reverse-vocal swells, subtle time-stretched ambient beds under breakdowns (PaulXStretch), tape-style delay throws on vocal chops, occasional cheeky one-shot (siren, vinyl spin). Mix glue: light EQ>comp>limit bus chain, prominent sidechain pump on bass/pads gives the whole track its breathing bounce.
MOOD: Playful, cheeky, celebratory — groove-first fun rather than dark or aggressive. Arc: intro groove (kick+hats+perc) → bass riff enters → hook riff answers → breakdown strips to filtered chords/vocal + swell → snap-back drop where bass + hook interlock at full bounce. Tension is built by filtering and subtraction, never by distortion or dissonance. Consistently bright-feeling even in minor keys.
HOOK-ID: The rubbery, filter-wobbling bass riff interlocked with a pitch-shifted 'talkbox-alien' vocal phrase over a clean punchy 4/4 — the bassline IS the melody, ducking around the kick with a sidechained bounce; listeners identify a Dom Dolla record from that wobbling bass-and-vocal riff alone within two bars.

### Calvin Harris
BPM: Recent anthems run 128-143: Blessings 130 BPM (B minor), Desire 140, Miracle 143 (A major, trance-tempo). Classic piano-house mode (One Kiss lineage, Blessings feel) sits 124-130 — for the app's 120-128 house bucket, target 124-128 and the palette translates perfectly. Funk Wav material is ~100-110.
DRUMS: Immaculate four-on-the-floor: round, punchy, tuned kick (never distorted), tight offbeat open hats carrying the drive, crisp 909-style claps/snares on 2 and 4, light 16th closed-hat fills and shaker top loops. Essentially straight (no swing) — energy comes from the sidechain pump, not shuffle. Fills are simple snare rolls + hat lifts into drops; everything mixed clean and radio-loud with lots of headroom per element. Funk Wav mode swaps to lazier, dustier live-feel drums with organic percussion.
BASS: Two modes. (1) Anthem mode: rolling sidechained bass pumping against the kick — either a warm sub + saw layer playing driving 8ths/16ths on the root, or his signature 'bounce' supersaw bass stab (detuned saws, filtered, short and rubbery). The pump (heavy sidechain compression ducking bass/pads to the kick) IS the groove. (2) Funk Wav mode: live-sounding slap/fingered funk bass, syncopated and melodic, sitting way forward in the mix.
CHORDS/PADS: Big bright piano chords are the core: rolling piano octaves and stabbed major/uplifting-minor triads (house-piano lineage of One Kiss, front and center in Miracle and Blessings), doubled by wide detuned supersaw pads and airy analog-style strings. Voicings are simple, open, diatonic — 4-chord loops with strong tonic pull. Everything sidechained to the kick for the euphoric pump. Funk Wav mode: Rhodes/Clavinet, wah guitar licks, vintage boogie keys.
LEAD/HOOK: The vocal topline is always king (he casts big-name vocalists), but instrumentally the hook is carried by: the piano riff itself (Miracle's rolling piano-octave figure, Blessings' bright piano-house chords), plucky trance arps in bright major keys, and shimmering supersaw lead layers that answer the vocal in the drop. Drops are 'airy' — the hook melody restated on stacked saws + piano over the pump rather than a bass-noise wall.
FX/TEXTURE: Long white-noise risers and reverse-cymbal swells into every drop; filtered-noise downlifters after; wide shimmer reverb on pianos/vocal chops; short delay throws on vocal phrases; high-pass filter builds (whole mix opens up at the drop); glittery one-shot sparkles. Signature move: the pre-drop breath — everything but voice + piano cuts, one bar of silence/riser, then the full pumping drop. Polish level is extreme: clean, warm, zero grit.
MOOD: Pure euphoria — sun-soaked, hands-in-the-air, major-key or bright-minor optimism. Arc: intimate piano+vocal intro → building pump with rising noise → wide-open airy drop → breakdown that strips to piano → bigger second drop. Never dark, never aggressive; 'fluffy, sun-soaked dance anthem' is the critical consensus language for Blessings. Funk Wav mode is laid-back beach-BBQ warmth.
HOOK-ID: The euphoric sidechain pump under rolling piano: a bright, simple piano riff (octaves or stabbed triads) breathing in time with a round four-on-floor kick, answered by wide detuned supersaw layers, with a noise-riser + one-bar breath before an airy, hook-restating drop. Hear pumping piano + supersaw sunshine at 124-143 BPM with radio-grade polish and you name Calvin Harris in one bar.

### MK (Marc Kinchen)
BPM: 120-128 BPM; 'Dior' verified at 128, classic MK material sits 122-126. Dead center of the house/tech-house bucket.
DRUMS: Clean, punchy four-on-the-floor house kick — solid and round, never distorted or hard-clipped. Crisp closed hi-hats running 8ths/16ths with THE defining MK groove trick (from his own MusicRadar masterclass): TWO hi-hat lines swung at DIFFERENT percentages against each other, producing his rolling shuffle. Offbeat open hats. Tight clap/snare on 2 and 4, often layered clap+snare. Light percussion garnish only (shaker, rim, occasional conga). Everything dead-center panned and tight — he explicitly never pans hats ('loosens the drum sound'). Lows cut from hats, no muffle. Feel: shuffled, bouncy, hypnotic — moderate swing (roughly 55-60% MPC-style), never stiff, never breakbeat.
BASS: Warm, rounded, BOUNCY deep-house bass — the lineage is the Korg M1 'Organ 2' organ-bass patch (his 90s trademark, root + fifth sine-organ stack), modernized with Moog-style analog roundness or Sylenth/Massive soft-saw subs. Rhythm: syncopated offbeat/16th-note pumping stabs locked to the kick pockets — short plucked notes that bounce, not sustained drones, not rolling reese, not wobble. Follows chord roots with small octave hops. Highs rolled off (he EQs hiss out of every bass) so it sits fat and smooth under the piano. 'MK's signature deep basslines' is literally the critical shorthand used for Dior.
CHORDS/PADS: Warm 90s house piano and M1-style organ stabs — 'summery piano stabs' is the standing critical description. Minor 7th/9th deep-house voicings, mid-register, played as short rhythmic comps (offbeat skank or push-pull anticipations), occasionally sustained into soft dub chords. Behind them: soft analog pads (Juno-style) and sparse string washes for choruses. Harmony is soulful and slightly bittersweet — minor-key but sunny (Dior charted around E minor/C major at 128).
LEAD/HOOK: THE fingerprint: pitched, chopped VOCAL-SAMPLE STABS used as the lead melodic instrument. Syllables sliced from a vocal, re-pitched to the chord tones, and re-sequenced into a stuttering rhythmic hook — the 'MK dub' technique he invented on the 'Push the Feeling On' remix and that still carries Dior's drop in 2025. Chart-record structure: sung topline in verses, then the drop = the vocal chopped into a percussive melody over the piano/bass groove. Secondary hooks come from the piano stab riff itself. For an instrumental kids palette: a formant-y 'vocal chop' synth patch (short vowel stabs, ooh/eh/ah) playing a bouncy 1-bar riff over piano stabs replicates it exactly.
FX/TEXTURE: Restrained and clean — MK's mixes are dry, tight, and subtractive-EQ'd rather than FX-drenched. Tasteful tempo-synced delay throws and short plate reverb on the vocal chops, gentle low-pass filter sweeps into/out of sections, small white-noise risers and a filtered-down breakdown before each drop (breakdown = piano + vocal, drums out, then hats shuffle back in). Wide stacked backing-vocal pads (he layers up to 16 BV tracks panned wide) while all rhythm elements stay mono-center. No supersaw walls, no big-room impacts.
MOOD: Warm, soulful, sunny-with-a-hint-of-longing; groove-led rather than drop-led. Energy arc is a gentle wave: shuffling intro groove, verse, soft filtered breakdown, then a drop that ADDS bounce (chopped vocal + full bass) rather than slamming. Smooth, danceable, feel-good — club music your whole family nods to, never aggressive.
HOOK-ID: Pitched, chopped vocal-sample stabs as the LEAD instrument — a stuttering re-pitched syllable riff bouncing over warm 90s piano/organ stabs and a round syncopated deep-house bass with double-swung shuffling hats. One bar of that combination and listeners have said 'MK' since 1992 — and a UK #1 in July 2025 proves the same fingerprint still tops the pop chart.

### Fred again..
BPM: Core house/garage output sits ~124-135 BPM; the 122-128 window for the app bucket is squarely in his house lane (a few outliers run 140+ toward UK bass/dnb — ignore those for this palette).
DRUMS: Soft, rounded, slightly dusty house kick (lo-fi layered, never hard EDM), with the whole kit audibly breathing under sidechain pump. Two rhythmic modes: (a) 4-on-the-floor house at light-to-moderate swing, (b) UKG/2-step — skipping syncopated kick, snare/clap on 2&4, shuffled swung 16th hats (moderate-to-heavy garage shuffle, ~55-62% swing feel). Hats are crisp but quiet in the mix; shaker layers and FOUND-SOUND foley percussion (recorded table knocks, clicks, mouth sounds) fill the top end instead of bright cymbals. Claps are soft, wide, often layered with a vinyl-crackle transient.
BASS: Warm round sub / low-mid analog-style bass, simple and supportive: sustained or gently rolling root notes locked under the chords, heavily sidechained so it pumps/breathes with the kick. On garage-leaning cuts it turns bouncy and syncopated (short plucked sub notes answering the kick). Never distorted, never aggressive reese — always soft and cushiony.
CHORDS/PADS: The emotional core: warm DETUNED felt-piano / electric-piano chords — 7ths, 9ths, add2s, gospel/pop-leaning four-chord loops (vi-IV-I-V / i-VI-III-VII type motion) — with tape wobble and slight pitch drift, washed in long hall reverb. Behind them: drone/pad beds made from time-stretched vocal or found-sound material, plus airy synth pad swells. Everything long-held gets deep sidechain pump so the harmony 'inhales' on every beat.
LEAD/HOOK: The hook is a chopped, pitch-shifted HUMAN VOICE — voice-note/FaceTime-style vocal fragments sliced into short melodic phrases, formant-shifted up and down, resampled, doubled, drenched in reverb/delay; the voice is the lead instrument, restated by a simple piano motif. For an instrumental kids palette: emulate with a 'vocal chop' instrument (formant-shifted ooh/ah choir chops or a vowel-y synth) playing short 2-4 note pentatonic-leaning phrases that repeat and answer the piano.
FX/TEXTURE: Continuous vinyl/tape crackle and room-tone beds (the track never sits on digital silence); reverse-reverb swells into downbeats; long reverb throws on phrase endings; soft filter sweeps and gentle white-noise risers (never screechy); sidechain-pump depth itself used as a transition device (ducking deepens into the drop); breakdown moments of near-silence with only crackle + a lone piano/voice before the groove re-enters; occasional field-recording ambience (crowd murmur, street noise).
MOOD: Bittersweet euphoria: intimate, diary-like and lo-fi in verses, blooming into warm communal uplift at the drop. Builds are gradual swells (pads + crackle + pump deepening), not EDM riser-drops. Dynamics constantly breathe. Melancholy-tinged chords carried by cozy, hopeful energy.
HOOK-ID: A chopped, pitch/formant-shifted 'voice-note' vocal fragment used as the lead melodic instrument, riding over warm detuned emotional piano chords with deep sidechain pump and an ever-present vinyl-crackle/foley bed — three seconds of that combination reads unmistakably as Fred again.., the most imitated production signature of the 2020s.

### Disco Lines
BPM: 121-131 verified range: 'Baby Girl' 121 BPM (D major, Tunebat), core catalogue ~124-128, 'No Broke Boys' 131 BPM (C#/Db minor, Tunebat). For a house-bucket palette, 124-128 is the sweet spot.
DRUMS: Punchy four-on-the-floor house kick — round, warm, lightly saturated, tight ~55 Hz thump with a short tail so the sidechain pump reads clearly. Bright 90s-house clap/snap layered on 2 & 4, offbeat open hats, 16th closed hats with light swing (~54-58%), shaker/tambourine loops driving the top end. Percussion is sparing and playful: conga/rim accents, occasional disco tom fills and crash washes into sections. The groove reads as 'bounce' — it comes more from bass syncopation + pump than from heavy shuffle. EDM.com on 'MDMA': 'airy blend of subtle percussion'.
BASS: The signature engine: a chunky, rubbery, mid-forward BOUNCE bassline — filtered saw/square pluck with envelope/LFO filter wobble, or a looped picked funk/disco bass — playing syncopated, octave-hopping patterns that land on the offbeats, hard-sidechained to the kick. In the newer tech-house crossovers ('No Broke Boys', 131 BPM) it straightens into a rolling, driving pluck bass. Never a dark reese or aggressive growl — always elastic and cheerful.
CHORDS/PADS: Filtered disco/funk sample material: chopped rhythm-guitar loops (he explicitly cites sampling guitars but 'always keeps it to house'), string/brass stabs, plucky piano/EP chord stabs, plus airy 'effervescent' major-key pads and lush reverbed deep-house Rhodes-style chords on the dreamier cuts (e.g. 'MDMA'). Progressions are simple, happy, major/pentatonic — filter automation (LP/HP sweeps) does the arrangement work.
LEAD/HOOK: Pitched-up 'chipmunk-soul' vocal chops are the primary hook carrier — bubbly, bright, resampled soul/pop phrases arranged as a riff. On the big collabs a full pop topline (Tinashe on 'No Broke Boys', Morgan Harvill, MAX) carries the verse while the vocal-chop riff answers in the drop; occasionally a simple bright synth stab riff doubles the chop. Drops are groove-outs (bass + chops + drums), not big-room saw walls.
FX/TEXTURE: Exaggerated pumping sidechain as an audible aesthetic (the whole mix breathes on the kick); LP/HP filter sweeps into and out of drops; white-noise risers and reverse-cymbal builds; big reverb throws on vocal phrases; tape-stop/pitch-drop transitions; crowd 'hey' chants and party ad-libs; a warm vinyl-ish disco sheen over the sample layers. Builds are short and functional — energy stays up rather than cinematic.
MOOD: Relentlessly upbeat, sunny, party-positive — his own 'good good' brand ('bring smiles to dance floors everywhere' is the stated label mission). Arc: short filtered intro → quick build → bouncy euphoric drop → breakdown with pads/vocal → bigger second drop. Euphoric and cheeky, never dark, never aggressive; Billboard describes the No Broke Boys treatment as 'bright and bouncy electronic flourishes'.
HOOK-ID: A pitched-up chipmunk-soul vocal-chop riff bouncing over a chunky, syncopated, offbeat-hopping bassline and a filtered disco/funk loop, with the whole mix pumping visibly on an exaggerated kick sidechain — the instantly-recognizable 'good good' happy bounce.

### Purple Disco Machine
BPM: 118-124 BPM, sweet spot 120-122 ('In Your Arms' deconstructed at exactly 120). Fits the app's 120-128 house bucket at its lower, grooviest end.
DRUMS: Tight, round four-on-the-floor kick at moderate weight (punchy, warm, never distorted or boomy); intros often high-pass the kick and open the low end at the first section change. 8th-note closed hats keep the motor running, offbeat open hats add lift. Backbeat = handclaps layered with a reverb-tailed snare on 2 & 4 (claps sometimes hard-panned for width). The real signature is the LIVE-disco percussion bed on top: congas, tambourine 16ths, shaker — busy but light. Ear candy: resonant analogue 'zap' disco toms with pitch-drop envelopes as fills, and military snare-roll fills into drops. Groove is mostly straight (machine-tight kick/hat grid, light 5-10% shuffle at most on percussion) — the funk comes from bass/guitar syncopation, not hat swing.
BASS: THE identifier: octave-popping funk disco bass — running 8ths where the second of each 8th-note pair jumps to the octave above, constantly pulling energy upward. Sound is a springy, slightly synthy Moog-style mono bass or slap-bass hybrid: fast attack, short-decay 'popping' envelope, a little filter resonance, tight and dry, gently sidechained to the kick. Alternate mode: straight Giorgio Moroder arpeggio 8ths. Always melodic and bouncy, never a static sub drone.
CHORDS/PADS: Nile Rodgers/Chic-style chucked rhythm guitar — percussive single-chord 16th-note chops, thin/bright, panned to one side — is the harmonic motor. Under it: gospel/disco piano riffs (7th/9th voicings, often a sampled-feel 8-bar loop), Salsoul-style string section swells filling gaps between hook phrases, echoed string stabs punctuating downbeats, and warm analogue polysynth pads. Harmony is simple, sunny, loop-based: 2-4 chord minor-7/major progressions over 8 or 16 bars.
LEAD/HOOK: On record the hook is a pop vocal topline; instrumentally the hook duty rotates between (1) bright Italo-disco synth lead motifs — slightly detuned saw/square, 4-8 note earworm phrases with tape echo, (2) talkbox/vocoder phrases (his P-funk boogie flavour), (3) string-stab riffs, and (4) horn stabs. For a sound-alike palette, a sparkling Italo synth lead trading with string stabs over the octave bass carries the identity perfectly without any vocal.
FX/TEXTURE: French-touch filter moves: high-pass the whole mix or the kick in intros/breakdowns, then open it for the drop. White-noise/sweep risers into each drop; tape-echo throws on synth and stab phrases; phaser slow-sweeping a percussion loop for 8 bars; reverb-drenched snare accents; disco-tom pitch-drop zaps; crash-with-echo-tail section exits. All transitions are smooth and euphoric — no impacts, no braams, no aggressive downshifters.
MOOD: Sunny, euphoric, nostalgic feel-good — the brightest palette in mainstream house. Arc: filtered intro (drums + string stabs) -> layers add one at a time (piano -> bass -> guitar) -> full groove -> breakdown stripping to piano/strings over filtered drums -> snare-roll + riser build -> big euphoric final section. Energy is constant-bounce rather than tension-and-release; nothing dark, nothing aggressive.
HOOK-ID: The octave-bouncing funk bass (low-HIGH low-HIGH 8ths) locked with Chic-style chucked rhythm guitar and sparkling Italo string/synth stabs over a sunny 120 BPM four-on-the-floor — one bar of that bass+guitar groove is enough for a listener to say "Purple Disco Machine".

### HUGEL (Florent Hugel, Marseille, France)
BPM: 120-126 BPM. Morenita 124 (Db major feel over minor vamp), I Adore You 120 (B minor), club/Beatport cuts typically 124-126. Sweet spot for a sound-alike: 123-125.
DRUMS: Punchy, rounded 4-on-the-floor house kick (tight, slightly saturated, not big-room boomy) at 120-126; crisp offbeat open hats + driving 16th shakers/maracas with light swing (~55-58% MPC feel); clap/snare on 2 and 4 layered with rimshots. The identity layer is LIVE Latin hand percussion riding on top: congas (tumbao patterns), bongos, timbale fills and rolls at section turns, guira scrapes, cowbell accents. Percussion frequently carries breakdowns alone (drums drop to congas+shaker before the bass re-enters).
BASS: Rolling tech-house bass — a round sub fused with a plucky, lightly saturated mid layer, playing syncopated offbeat/tumbao-flavoured patterns that lock between the kicks (never plain root 8ths for long); sidechained to the kick for a gentle pump. Hypnotic, insistent, groovy rather than aggressive — the 'monstrous rolling bassline under sunny percussion' contrast is core to the sound.
CHORDS/PADS: Deliberately sparse harmony: short piano/organ or muted-brass stab chords on offbeats rather than sustained pads; minor-key vamps of 3-4 chords (e.g. I Adore You: Bm-A-Em-G, i-VII-iv-VI at 120 BPM); occasional cumbia accordion vamps or Latin/Spanish guitar comping. Pads only as risers/atmosphere in builds — the mix leaves space for percussion and hook.
LEAD/HOOK: The hook is a short, chantable Latin melodic phrase, normally a vocal chant/loop (cumbia group chants, call-and-response); for instrumental use it translates directly to: cumbia accordion riff (Morenita), mariachi/salsa brass-section stabs and trumpet lines, marimba or Latin guitar licks, and whistle-like top synths. Hooks are 1-2 bar earworms repeated with filter/percussion variation, not long melodies.
FX/TEXTURE: Crowd 'hey!' shouts and whistles, timbale roll fills into drops, reverse cymbals, white-noise/filter risers (used sparingly), tape-style filter sweeps on the hook, drop-outs to percussion-plus-hook a cappella as the main transition; festive carnival ambience (party crowd, hand-clap layers). No dark or industrial textures anywhere in the palette.
MOOD: Sunny, communal, carnival-festive; medium-high energy sustained by groove rather than big-room drops. Arc: percussion-led intro, hook tease, rolling-bass groove sections, breakdown to chant/accordion + congas, timbale-roll build, full-groove drop. Feels like a beach fiesta, never tense or dark.
HOOK-ID: A 1-2 bar cumbia chant-style hook — rendered by accordion, brass stabs or marimba — riding a rolling, syncopated tech-house bassline with live congas/timbales/shakers over a tight four-on-the-floor: the instant 'carnival house' fingerprint he pioneered; the timbale-roll into a percussion-plus-hook drop-out is the giveaway transition.

### Peggy Gou
BPM: Nanana = 130 BPM (C minor original; edit listed G minor). Album spans roughly 115-130; core singles sit ~120-130, so the palette drops naturally into a 120-128 kids-app bucket without losing identity.
DRUMS: Warm 'analog-style drum machine' kit, essentially a friendly TR-909: round solid 4-on-the-floor 909 kick (clean, not distorted), 909 closed hats on 16ths with pronounced swing/shuffle (Resident Advisor: 'chunky, swung drums'), open hat on offbeats, soft 909 clap/snare on 2 and 4, light shaker and rim/conga accents. Groove is loose and roomy (~56-58% swing feel), deliberately retro-90s rather than quantized-modern.
BASS: Simple rolling minor-key analog synth bass: warm, round, slightly rubbery single-note 8th/16th ostinato locked to the kick (the Nanana pattern), sub-weighted but melodic and bouncy; reviewers single out her 'perfectly produced basslines'. Occasionally flips disco/R&B-leaning (I Believe in Love Again). Never aggressive or growly.
CHORDS/PADS: THE signature: Korg M1 sounds (she publicly named the M1 her favourite synth and confirmed the album 'including Nanana' was recorded with it) — classic 90s house M1 organ stabs and M1 piano chords, plus 'vintage rave chords' (RA) voiced as minor 7th/9th stabs.
